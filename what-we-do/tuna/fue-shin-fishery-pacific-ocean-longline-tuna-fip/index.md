<h2>Overview</h2>

In September 2026, <a href="https://fisheries.msc.org/en/fisheries/fue-shin-fishery-fsf-wcpo-longline-albacore-yellowfin-and-bigeye-fishery/@@view"target="_blank">Fue Shin Fishery (FSF) achieved Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) certification</a> for its Western and Central Pacific Ocean longline albacore, yellowfin, and bigeye tuna fishery, marking a major milestone in its sustainability journey and the successful completion of its Fishery Improvement Project (FIP).

Launched in April 2020, the Fue Shin Fishery Pacific Ocean Longline Tuna FIP was a collaborative effort between FSF and O2 to meet the rising global demand for tuna in a sustainable manner by assuring catches do not exceed sustainable levels, promoting an ecosystem-based approach to fisheries management, and strengthening policy and governance systems in the Pacific Ocean region. The fishery is managed regionally by the Western and Central Pacific Fisheries Commission (WCPFC) in the Western and Central Pacific Ocean and by the Inter American Tropical Tuna Commission (IATTC) in the Eastern Pacific Ocean (EPO). 

This FIP was part of a larger effort O2 is pursuing across East Asia to help lead improvement projects and certification in the longline tuna fishing sector. This FIP was one of two projects between FSF and O2 focused on longline tuna sustainability — [the other is for longline tuna vessels in the Indian Ocean.] (/what-we-do/tuna/fue-shin-fishery-indian-ocean-longline-tuna-fip/)

---

##What Our Work Focused On:  

* Contributed to a strategy, timeline and process to rebuild EPO yellowfin and bigeye tuna stocks targeted in the FIP.
* Worked to define and implement robust, well-managed precautionary harvest strategies and harvest control rules and tools for all four tuna species.
* Ensured the fishery improves the quality, verification and provision of its catch data and interactions with endangered, protected and threatened species. This ultimately supported the science and management decision at the WCPFC and IATTC and helped quantify the fishery’s impact.
* Verified effective grievance mechanisms were accessible to all workers onboard vessels and made efforts to make crew aware of their rights.
* Assessed social risks in the fishery against globally recognized best practices and integrated opportunities to address human and labor rights in fishery improvement processes.
* Provided technical support and oversight for implementation of both environmental and social work, including progress tracking and reporting transparently on FisheryProgress.org.
